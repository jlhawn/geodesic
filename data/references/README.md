# Observational references

Each reference the scorecards compare the model with is a file here that records where it comes from: the data set with its DOI or URL and license, the period, the box, the mask, the definition (including the model quantity that matches it), and the script that wrote it. A row of a scorecard takes its target from one of these files, never from a number typed into a script.

| file | quantity | source | period | script |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ceres-ebaf-global-monthly.json` | global TOA incoming solar, all-sky and clear-sky outgoing fluxes, cloud radiative effects | CERES-EBAF Ed4.2.1 (obs4MIPs), doi:10.5067/TERRA-AQUA-NOAA20/CERES/EBAF_L3B004.2.1, CC BY 4.0 | 2001–2024 | `scripts/referenceCeres.mjs` |
| `era5-fields-1991-2020.bin` (`.json`) | monthly 2 m temperature 30–70°N, sea-level pressure and eastward surface stress 10°S–10°N, on 1° | ERA5, C3S/ECMWF, via NCAR RDA ds633.1, doi:10.5065/P8GT-0R61, CC BY 4.0 | 1991–2020 | `scripts/referenceEra5Fields.mjs` |
| `global-ts-era5.json` | global mean surface temperature by month in the model's definition, with the 2 m version and the gap | ERA5, C3S/ECMWF, via NCAR RDA ds633.1, doi:10.5065/P8GT-0R61, CC BY 4.0 | 1991–2020 | `scripts/referenceTs.mjs` |
| `gpcp-precip-1991-2020.bin` (`.json`) | monthly precipitation on 2.5° | GPCP v2.3 (NOAA PSL long-term mean), doi:10.7289/V56971M6, public | 1991–2020 | `scripts/referenceGpcp.mjs` |
| `nsidc-sea-ice-monthly.json` | Arctic and Antarctic sea-ice extent and area by month | NSIDC Sea Ice Index v4 (G02135), doi:10.7265/a98x-0f50, no restrictions | 1991–2020 | `scripts/referenceSeaIce.mjs` |

| `woa23-sst-1991-2020.bin` (`.json`) | monthly SST on 1° | World Ocean Atlas 2023, 1991–2020 normal (decav91C0), doi:10.25923/54bh-1613, public domain | 1991–2020 | `scripts/packWoaMonthlySst.py … decav91C0` |

`scripts/references.mjs` reads them for the scorecards: it checks each binary file against the sha256 its .json records and every file against its shape, and samples the grids (`test/references.test.mjs`).

Definitions that the earlier scorecards got wrong, as the Nov 7 audit found them:

- **Clear sky.** EBAF's `rsutcs` and `rlutcs` are the clear-sky fluxes of cloud-free areas. The model's clear-sky pass removes the clouds from every column, which is EBAF's total-region clear sky; Loeb et al. (2020) put that 2.2 W/m² lower in OLR and 0.5 W/m² higher in reflected sunlight. The CERES file's `rstcre` and `rltcre` are EBAF's effects against the total-region clear sky (annual −45.13 and 25.70 W/m², against −45.61 and 27.80 from the cloud-free fluxes); score the model against those.
- **SST period.** WOA23's `decav` averages the atlas's decades and is centered near 1988; the ocean's initial state (`data/woa_march_1deg.bin`) and the SST restoring and fixed-SST targets (`data/woa_sst_monthly_1deg.bin`) both take it. Score SST against the 1991–2020 normal here, 0.20 K warmer over 30S–30N.
- **Global surface temperature.** The 14.85 °C target had no source. ERA5's skin temperature (skt) is the grid box's skin: at a point with sea ice it weighs the ice's skin and the open water of its leads by cover. The reference takes skt on land (land-sea mask ≥ 0.5) and at sea points with ice, and the SST on open sea: 15.26 °C for the year (15.259 with each month weighted by its mean length over 1991–2020; the plain mean of the twelve months is 15.251), 13.24 in January to 17.15 in July. The model's matching quantity is not its `surfaceT` but `means.gridBoxSkin` (`js/intervalMeans.module.js`): c·surfaceT + (1 − c)·sst on sea cells with ice cover c, surfaceT elsewhere, taken sample by sample. With 2 m air in place of the skin, the station records' definition, it is 15.21 °C; the skin less the air is +0.05 K for the year, −0.07 K in January to +0.16 K in August, since land skin runs below its air in winter (−0.36 K in January) and above it in summer (+0.46 K in July), and at sea points with ice the grid-box skin runs 0.2–0.7 K above the grid-box 2 m air. Berkeley Earth's Land/Ocean climatology for 1991–2020 (2 m air on land and sea ice, SST elsewhere) is 14.76 °C, computed from its Land_and_Ocean file as the file's 1951–1980 absolute with air temperatures above sea ice, 14.102 °C, plus the file's mean anomaly over 1991–2020, 0.658 K. That is 0.45 K below ERA5's same definition: the data sets differ by more than the definitions do. The Berkeley file is not kept here, since ERA5 matches the model's quantity and Berkeley's CC BY-NC license limits use.
- **2 m temperature.** The 40–60°N land row compared the model's lowest layer, about 20 m up, with ERA5's 2 m air, from 2010–2019 and March–September only. It now takes `t2m` from `era5-fields-1991-2020.bin`, but on the model side it still compares the lowest layer's air at that layer's own height, which is not definition-matched: the matching quantity is a 2 m air temperature diagnostic, which the surface-layer work (bl-surface) is building. The reference samples ERA5 at each model land cell from the four surrounding points weighted by `lsm` (by 1 − `lsm` at a sea cell), so a coastal cell takes the points of its own kind; over the N = 64 land of 40–60°N this moves the reference by +0.01 K in JJA and −0.09 K in DJF from plain bilinear sampling.
- **Equatorial stress and pressure.** The earlier rows sampled ERA5 on four days a month in 2007–2019. `era5-fields-1991-2020.bin` holds the monthly means of every hour, 1991–2020; score the run's own stress (its exchange, not a rebuilt one) and sea-level pressure against `metss` and `msl` on the model's sea cells, each sampled from ERA5's points weighted by 1 − `lsm`.
- **Undercurrent.** `tao-adcp-equatorial-undercurrent.json` gives each mooring's profile as the mean of its twelve calendar-month means, so a record heavy in some seasons does not bias it: cores 0.50 m/s at 188 m (165°E), 0.70 at 153 m (170°W), 1.08 at 106 m (140°W) and 0.95 at 75 m (110°W). A depth is kept only where every calendar month has at least 30 days from at least 10 distinct years, so every listed depth is climatological: 30–265 m at 165°E, 25–285 m at 170°W, 25–270 m at 140°W and 25–250 m at 110°W. The shallower and deeper bins, which only a few years sample, are left out.
- **Precipitation.** `gpcp-precip-1991-2020.bin` is the file the earlier scorecards read from scratch (byte for byte), now with its provenance: global mean 2.69 mm/day.
- **Sea ice.** The passive-microwave index counts no lake ice, and its monthly values are means of daily values, each day's extent and area cut at 15 %. The model's matching quantities are the same daily cuts of each day's mean concentration over its sea cells (neither land nor lake), which `spinup.mjs` logs at each day's end to 0.01 Mkm², averaged over the window's days; a cut of the 30-day mean concentration is a different quantity. pmin's Arctic extent at N = 128 over the 30 days ending at the March equinox, against a day-weighted 14.97 Mkm²: on the daily cut, +0.32 Mkm² with its FLake ice (0.32 Mkm² of lake ice) and +0.00 for the sea ice alone; on the 30-day-mean cut the earlier scorecard used, +0.42 and +0.07. The index's extent counts the Arctic pole hole as ice, but its area leaves the hole out, and the hole shrank from 0.31 Mkm² (SSM/I) to 0.029 (SSMIS) in January 2008. The file's `area` adds each month's hole at full concentration (0.188 Mkm² on average over 1991–2020) and `areaPublished` keeps the index's values. Score the model's daily area (concentration times cell area over its sea cells at least 15 % covered, the polar cap included) against `area`.
