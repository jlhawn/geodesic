# The ALE vertical grid for the ocean

Design for replacing the bulk mixed layer over isopycnal classes with an arbitrary-Lagrangian–Eulerian (ALE) vertical grid in the MOM6 sense. Every layer is a TRiSK shallow-water layer as today and the dynamics step is Lagrangian: layers move with the flow and their thickness follows continuity. After each step the column is **regridded** to a target grid produced by a generator, and the water is **conservatively remapped** onto that target. The generator is a pluggable piece: pure isopycnal (today's coordinate, which the ALE core must reproduce), z* (fixed depths everywhere), and the hybrid of section 2 (fixed-depth near the surface and wherever the water is weakly stratified, isopycnal wherever it is stratified) are three generators on one core, and they can be run against each other. The hybrid is the intended production generator. Vertical physics becomes a column problem on the regridded layers, as the atmosphere's already is.

Decided 2026-10-15. The repairs to the slab (stress reach, light promotion, interior exchange) are not pursued; the ocean-only ladder and the WOA-restored loop remain measuring instruments for judging the new ocean against the old.

## 1. What stays and what goes

Stays, unchanged or with an index offset: the TRiSK layer dynamics in vector-invariant form, the split barotropic free surface and its sub-stepping, the ∇⁴ closure, the equation of state, the Gent–McWilliams interface-height transport, the coupling contract with the atmosphere, the ice and the skin (`capacity`, `transmission`, `penetrated`, `oceanFlux`, the virtual salt flux), the recorded-forcing and ocean-only machinery, the state-file plumbing.

Gone (removed 2026-10-18): the bulk mixed layer as a special layer 0 and everything that served it: the convective swallow and erode loop, shear and stirring entrainment, the Monin–Obukhov retreat with its daily and light gates, refill floors, `neutralSnap`, `mixedNeighbourRatio`, `carryMomentum`'s homogenization, the stress body force over `max(momentumDepth, h₀)`, `windDepths`, the Ekman block and `upwellingSource`, the token-layer rules (EPS tokens, THIN visibility, the token velocity relaxation, `closureFill`'s rings), `restoreDensities`, `penetrationShares`' receiver search, and the `interfacialDrag` piston. About 370 CPU lines and 300 WGSL lines, and roughly 1,900 of the ocean test lines.

New: the ALE core (regrid to a generator's target, conservative remap), the three generators, a column vertical-mixing scheme with an implicit solve, a pressure force for layers whose density varies horizontally, per-layer sunlight deposition, surface stress as a flux, a state converter.

## 2. The coordinate

### 2.1 Layers

K layers, each with a label density ρ_k (as `LAYER_DENSITIES` today) **and** a minimum thickness Δz_k (new), whose running sum z_k = Σ_{j<k} Δz_j is the z grid a column takes where every layer is held at its minimum. Each layer carries thickness, heat h·T and salt h·S per cell and normal velocity per edge, as today. No layer has a special role; the surface layer is simply k = 0.

The label list extends the present 46 classes upward with light labels that no seawater reaches, whose only purpose is to be surface layers everywhere. The table as built in stage 1 (`hybridLayerTable` in `js/ocean/column.module.js`):

| k | ρ_k | Δz_k (m) | z_k reaches | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–11 | 1016.5 … 1019.25 (0.25 apart) | 3 growing 8 % a layer to 7 | 57 m | surface layers, fixed-depth everywhere |
| 12–20 | 1019.5 … 1021.5 (the present five) plus 1021.25, 1021.625, 1021.75, 1021.875 | 8 … 10 | 144 m | warm pool; fixed-depth wherever the surface water is denser than the label, as it is outside the fresh tropics |
| 21–43 | 1022.0 … 1026.6 (the present 23, 0.25 then 0.1 apart) | 10 each | 374 m | thermocline and intermediate water; isopycnal wherever the isopycnals are more than 10 m apart |
| 44–57 | 1026.65 … 1026.98 (the present twelve), 1027.1, 1027.5 | 40 growing to 500 | 3006 m | deep and bottom water; a weakly stratified column is a z grid to about 3 km |

58 layers; the GPU's layer mask allows 64 (`js/gpu/layeredOcean.gpu.js:55`). Memory at N=128 rises from about 1.54 GB to about 1.9 GB (six state copies of K(3C+E) floats plus scratch); at N=64 from about 390 MB to about 480 MB.

The first draft had the minima growing 8 % a layer through the whole table (15 to 65 m across the thermocline labels). On a subtropical profile (28 °C to 100 m, then 23 °C e-folding over 250 m) that pushed every thermocline interface into fixed-depth mode, because the cumulative floor outran the isopycnals: the whole upper kilometer became a z grid, which is what the coordinate exists to avoid. With 10 m minima the same profile is isopycnal from the 1024.25 label down (the lighter thermocline labels sit 10 m apart as a z grid at 144–234 m, good resolution for the equatorial thermocline and undercurrent), and the deep labels down to the bottom water's density are isopycnal to within a tenth of a layer.

### 2.2 The target grid (regrid)

For each column, after the dynamics have moved the layers, compute the target interface depths ẑ_k (`targetInterfaces`):

1. **Isopycnal position.** From the column's current profile, the depth d_k at which the potential density (referenced to the surface, the same density the labels use) crosses the boundary half-way between labels k−1 and k. The layer means are held from decreasing with depth (a running maximum) and reconstructed with the remap's own limited parabolas, and the crossing is found cell by cell; a boundary that falls in the jump at an interface puts d_k at that interface. Linear interpolation between layer centers was the first draft and is not a fixed point: between two uniform layers of unequal thickness it puts the crossing (h_k − h_{k−1})/4 off the interface, so a column already on target would be regridded and remapped every step. The parabolas alone are not a fixed point either, since the interface value between two uniform layers is not the label midpoint. The coordinate's own modes settle it: a layer whose two interfaces were both isopycnal at the last regrid is by construction a uniform class, so its reconstruction is flattened by the smaller of their mode weights (`previousWeight`), while fixed-depth layers, which hold a gradient, keep the full parabola. A column of uniform layers at their labels is then exactly on target, and the transition from fixed to isopycnal mode is continuous.
2. **Minimum thickness.** ẑ_k = max(d_k, ẑ_{k−1} + Δz_k): HYCOM's rule, cumulative per layer, not the absolute minimum depth of the first draft. A layer whose isopycnal spacing is thinner than its minimum is held at its minimum thickness and holds whatever water lies there (fixed-depth mode); one whose isopycnals are further apart follows them (isopycnal mode). Then ẑ_k ≤ D + η and every layer keeps at least h_min = 0.5 m, the interfaces being spaced from the top and clipped from the floor, so no token layers arise and in columns shallower than the table the layers whose minimum depth lies below the floor collapse to h_min at the bottom (HYCOM's vanishing layers); on a 50 m shelf the twelve surface layers are squeezed to about 27 m and the rest hold 23 m of h_min layers. A terrain-following variant for shallow columns (HYCOM's σ-z-ρ) is an open point below.
3. **Mode weight.** w_k = clamp((ẑ_k − ẑ_{k−1} − Δz_k)/δ, 0, 1) with δ = 5 m marks how isopycnal interface k is; it is used by GM, by the diagnostics and by the next regrid's flattening. The surface and the floor have w = 0.
4. **Rate limit.** An isopycnal interface moves toward its target by at most a fraction of the distance per step (MOM6's regrid filter), so the remap does not chase grid-scale noise: ẑ_k ← z_k^old + min(1, dt/τ_r)(ẑ_k − z_k^old), never above its floor; a held interface goes all the way. τ_r is open.

This is Bleck's (2002) hybrid generator, continuous rather than a hard seam. The same core serves the isopycnal generator (ẑ_k = d_k, every interior weight 1, so every layer is flat: today's coordinate with h_min in place of tokens) and the z* generator (the depth table stretched by (D + η)/D, every weight 0), and the three can be run against each other.

### 2.3 The remap

Conservative, monotone piecewise-parabolic (PPM) reconstruction of T and S on the old grid, integrated onto the new (`remapColumn`). Interior interface values are the slope at the interface of the quartic through the five running integrals of the four layers around it (fourth order on any spacing), the next-to-boundary ones the thickness-weighted mean of the two layers, the surface and floor values the boundary layers' own means (no boundary extrapolation, as MOM6's default); each interface value is held between the two means it separates and each layer's parabola is limited to lie between its interface values (Colella and Woodward 1984), so no new extremum appears. Thickness is set by the targets; heat and salt are the integrals, so water, heat and salt are conserved to rounding. Stage 1 measured: amounts conserved to 1e-12, means within the old extrema, a column on its own grid unchanged, a linear profile exact away from the boundary layers, and on a smooth profile the error falls twentyfold on halving the spacing (the linear form's thirteenfold). Velocity is remapped per edge column with the edge thicknesses (the mean of the two cells' before and after), piecewise-linear with the monotonized central slope (order 2 of the same routine), and `shiftVelocities` then restores the barotropic transport as it does today. The remap runs after `rescale` and before the column physics, once per ocean step.

## 3. Dynamics on the new layers

### 3.1 Pressure force

Today only layer 0 has a horizontally varying density. In the hybrid every fixed-depth layer does. The force on layer k at an edge between cells a and b is the hydrostatic gradient at fixed depth,

F_k = −[ g Δη + (g/ρ₀) Δ( P_k + ρ_k (z − H_k) ) ] / dc,

with P_k = Σ_{j<k} ρ_j h_j, H_k = Σ_{j<k} h_j and Δ the b−a difference, evaluated at the depth z midway through the depths the layer occupies in both cells (the overlap of [H_k, H_k + h_k] on the two sides). For a class at its label (ρ_k the same on both sides) this is today's Montgomery gradient whatever the interface slopes; for a layer at the same depths on both sides it is the layer-mean form ΔP_k − ρ̄_k ΔH_k + ½ h̄_k Δρ_k, today's mixed-layer term for the surface layer. The layer-mean form evaluated at the middle of the edge-mean layer range, the first draft, is exact only in those two cases; where a layer's depths in the two cells do not overlap, as at a vanished layer against a full one on a slope, it extrapolates one column's hydrostatic line far outside the layer, and a 3500 m seamount under a uniform stratification on Grid(16) produced a 9 cm/s current in a day. With each cell's layer line taken at the middle of the overlap, or of the gap between the two layers where they share no depths, and the vanished layers pinned as below, the same case gives 2 mm/s, the ordinary partial-cell residual of the layer means of one profile over different intervals (`test/hybridOcean.test.mjs`). A first version read the pressure at the gap depth from each column's whole profile instead; it made no difference once the vanished layers were pinned, and the Montgomery form is the right one for an isopycnal layer whose interfaces jump across the edge. The analytic finite-volume form of Adcroft, Hallberg and Harrison (2008) remains the next step if the coupled runs show pressure-gradient noise on shelves.

### 3.2 Potential vorticity and thickness floors

With no token layers, `vorticityCentring` applies uniformly, q = (ζ + f)/max(h_e, vorticityCentring·h̄, min(PV_FLOOR, Δz_k)): the floor is the layer's own minimum thickness up to PV_FLOOR, so the 3–10 m fixed-depth surface layers feel their full Coriolis force (a 20 m floor would cut the top layer's to a seventh) while thin isopycnal classes keep the slab's protection. The edge thickness of every layer is the smaller of its two cells', as the slab's classes, and the donor-limited mixed-layer flux rule becomes the rule for every layer, since any layer can be thin on one side. A layer no thicker than twice h_min at an edge follows the layer above: its tendency relaxes to that layer's velocity and the barotropic shift copies it, as the slab's tokens. This was found on the real bathymetry at N=32, not in the flat and seamount tests: with the mean edge thickness and their own pressure force, the vanished bottom layers of two cells whose floors differ by a kilometer sit at different depths, feel the whole pressure difference at the sill depth, reach the 5 m/s clamp within a day, and their spurious transport and forcing drive the barotropic mode to a blow-up at Drake Passage and the Indian ridges within ten days (the first gate run, `runs/hybrid/dead1-hybrid32.log`). Pinned, the atlas ocean at rest over the real topography adjusts from its steric start at up to 1 m/s on the first day and decays steadily with no clamped edge. The stress is a tendency on the top layer, stress/(ρ₀ h₀); the bottom drag acts on the part of each layer within `momentumDepth` of the floor, so a vanished bottom layer passes it on to the water above; there is no interfacial drag, the column solve's viscosity couples the layers.

### 3.3 Gent–McWilliams

The interface-height transport stays as it is, multiplied by the smaller of the two cells' mode weights for the interface (w_k = clamp((h_{k−1} − Δz_{k−1})/δ, 0, 1), the excess of the layer above over its minimum, so the weights need no storage), so fixed-depth interfaces carry nothing and isopycnal ones carry the full tapered flux. The surface taper over `eddyTaperDepth` can then shrink, since fixed-depth interfaces are already excluded; stage 2 decides by test.

### 3.4 Tracer advection and the surface layers' lateral mixing

The thickness flux at an edge stays donor-limited, so no layer flows out of a cell where it holds no water. The heat and salt it carries take the edge value of the κ = 1/3 upwind-biased scheme with Koren's limiter (`tracerAdvection: 'third'`, the hybrid's default; `'upwind'` is the slab's and its only value): from the upwind cell's value φ_u, the downwind cell's φ_d and the value φ_uu of the cell beyond the upwind cell along the edge's own line (`beyondCells`: the neighbor whose direction from the upwind cell is most nearly opposite to the downwind cell's, within 37° at N=8), φ_e = φ_u + ½ ψ(r)(φ_d − φ_u) with r the upwind over the central gradient, each over its own spacing, and ψ(r) = max(0, min(2r, (2 + r)/3, 2)). Where the beyond cell is land or either cell beyond the upwind one holds no water in the layer, φ_e = φ_u. The scheme is third order where the field is smooth and monotone everywhere (`test/hybridOcean.test.mjs`: the midpoint on a linear field, the QUICK value on a smooth one, the upwind value at an extremum, and on a smooth field under solid-body rotation at N=8 a tendency within 3 % of the exact one against upwind's 16 %), the one-dimensional class MOM6's PPM belongs to, and it replaces the first-order flux's implicit diffusion of about |u|Δx/2 along the flow, 3e4 m²/s under a 1 m/s jet at N=128. Both cells of an edge compute the same φ_e from the same loads, so the GPU's per-cell gather conserves heat and salt as before; the beyond cells and their edges ride in two hybrid-only slots (BEY, BED).

The fixed-depth surface layers (the first `table.fixed`, twelve, about the top 57 m) carry a lateral diffusion of heat and salt along the layer, the edge flux κ_e h_e (φ_b − φ_a)/d_e, with κ_e = min(`shearDiffusivityCap`, `shearDiffusivity` |ζ_e| d_e²), ζ_e the relative vorticity at the edge (the mean of its two vertices', the vertex vorticity the PV already uses) and d_e the cell spacing: Smagorinsky's scaling with the shear, standing in for the tropical instability waves and the mesoscale stirring that a 56 km grid does not grow, and vanishing where the flow is smooth. The defaults 0.2 and 4000 m²/s put about 3000 m²/s on the SEC–undercurrent shear at N=128 (|ζ| ≈ 5e-6 s⁻¹), the range the observed waves' heat flux into the cold tongue implies, and cap the ACC's and the western boundary currents' at 4000; the coefficient is not tuned to the SST. Explicit stability needs κ dt/d² small, and with κ ∝ |ζ| d² that is C |ζ| dt, under 1e-2 at every N. Every layer also carries HYCOM's background Laplacian tracer diffusion, κ = `lateralDiffusionSpeed` d_e (0.005 m/s, HYCOM's temdf2: 280 m²/s at N=128, 560 at N=64), added to the shear term where both act. Three N=64 ocean-only arms under the recorded forcing ran to the 5 m/s limit in the Sulu and Celebes seas within 20 to 70 days (`hybrid64t`, the limited flux alone, 66 clamps by day 70 with a 7 K scatter between neighboring surface cells; `hybrid64tb`, with the background and no shear term, 80 clamps by day 40; `hybrid64base`, all three pieces, 26 clamps by day 30), while the two pieces without the background held a year (`hybrid64td`, 0 clamps) and the coupled N=128 year with all three ran clean: that mode was the vanished-layer pump at the unresolved sills that the pin of 2026-10-18 removes (§3.2's note and row 5), not the diffusion, and with the pin the three-piece defaults run 180 days at N=64 with no clamp. `tracerAdvection: 'third'` is still refused without `shearDiffusivity` above zero, since the limited flux alone has not been run again with the pin. The slab path keeps its own mixed-layer diffusion and refuses all three options.

## 4. Column physics

Runs once per ocean step on each sea column after the remap, in this order (`mixColumn` in `js/ocean/column.module.js`; stage 1 built and tested it on one column).

1. **Surface forcing.** Net heat flux into layer 0 (capacity ρc_p h₀); sunlight deposited per layer from the Jerlov transmission differences across each layer's interfaces (`sunlightShares`; the light passing the floor stays in the bottom layer, so the shares sum to one), none below 300 m; freshwater as a virtual salt flux into layer 0; the stress as a momentum flux into layer 0 of each edge column.
2. **Convective adjustment** (`convectiveAdjustment`). Statically unstable neighbors are merged into uniform slabs, from the surface down and in the interior, and the potential energy each slab released is measured with the full equation of state; the surface slab's release E_c is the convective source of the next step.
3. **Boundary-layer mixing** (`boundaryLayer`). An energetics-based planetary boundary layer in the budget of Reichl and Hallberg (2018) with the sources of Kraus and Turner: the boundary-layer depth h is where the work of mixing the column over the step with the diffusivity κ v* z (1 − z/h)², v*³ = u*³ + E_c/(ρ₀ dt), equals the energy ρ₀ m* u*³ dt + n* E_c. The work is the potential energy the implicit mixing raises, summed interface by interface from the heat and salt it moves and the thermal expansion and haline contraction there (linearized, so cabbeling cannot feed it); each trial solves only the layers the depth reaches, with the diffusivity set as the elimination descends and the work summed as the substitution climbs back, and h is the root in log depth of (work − energy)/(work + energy), bounded and near ±1 away from the root, found by the Anderson–Björck secant to a part in 10⁶ in a bracket around the previous step's depth (half to twice it, three trials when it has moved by less than a factor two) or else between the floor and the whole column, the side where the work is within the energy taken: about nine trials a column against the 26 of a bisection to the same tolerance (stage 5). m* = 1.25 Λ / (1 + (h|f|/(0.5 u*))²), Λ the Langmuir factor of the existing `langmuirFactor` (cap 2); it matches MOM6 OM4's m* to within 20 % over u*/(|f|h) from 2 to 10 and falls faster for deeper layers. n* = 0.2 (MOM6's default). With `mstarScheme: 'rh18'` the energy is instead ρ₀ dt Λ (m*_N C + m*_S) u*³ + 0.06 E_c in the form of Reichl and Li (2019, equations 14 and 15): m*_N the neutral fit (c_N1 = 0.275, c_N2 = 8, c_N3 = −5), C = 1 − 0.667 B⁻/(B⁻ + 2 m*_N u*³/h) under a destabilizing surface buoyancy flux B⁻, m*_S = 0.2 (B⁺² h/(u*⁵ |f|))^0.4 under a stabilizing one B⁺, m* no more than 10, with B = g (α heat − β salt)/dt from the heat, light and salt the surface gave the top layer over the step: the change of the sea-surface temperature since the last step, the light that passed the top layer, the evaporation and rain, and the brine of the ice grown. Stage 1 measured: the work equals the energy to within the search; the depth at 1 m and 4 m layers agrees within 6 %; wind over a 50 m mixed layer above a 5 K jump entrains at the Kraus–Turner rate exactly (the base reaches 62 m against the predicted 62.1 m after four days at u* = 2 cm/s), the potential energy raised being the work less the 18 % that cabbeling in this quadratic equation of state releases; 20 K·m of cooling on a 0.05 K/m ramp convects to 31 m against the non-penetrative 28 m. Under a daily cycle (test/helpers/pwp.mjs's forcing: a half-sine of 250 W/m² mean, a constant dark loss of 0.6–0.8 of it, 45°N), the dawn depth at 2 m/s wind is 6 and 29 m against PWP's 4 and 23.5 m, and the daily range of the top layer within 10 %; at 8 m/s it is 42 m against PWP's 23.5 m, the expected difference between an energetic scheme and PWP's shear criterion, which has no wind-stirring term and barely feels the wind at this latitude. KPP (Large, McWilliams and Doney 1994) remains the alternative if the coupled runs call for it; the implicit solve is the same.
4. **Interior mixing** (`interiorMixing`). Below and beside the boundary layer the Pacanowski–Philander Richardson-number viscosity ν = ν₀/(1 + 5Ri)² + ν_b and diffusivity ν/(1 + 5Ri) + κ_b, ν₀ = 1e-2, ν_b = 1e-4, κ_b = 1e-5 m²/s, with Ri from the resolved shear (the cell's reconstructed velocity) and stratification; a convective diffusivity of 0.1 m²/s where an interface is still unstable. The boundary-layer and interior diffusivities add; the viscosity is the boundary-layer diffusivity (Prandtl number 1) plus the interior one.
5. **The solve** (`solveColumn`). One implicit tridiagonal solve per column for T and S and, per edge column with the mean of the two cells' viscosity profiles, for u; the stress is a tendency on the top layer in the dynamics (`stress/(ρ₀ h₀)`, section 3.2), and this solve hands its momentum down. Bottom drag stays in the dynamics, applied over the bottom `momentumDepth` as today, since a drag on a vanished 0.5 m bottom layer would be absorbed there and never reach the water above. For the GPU twin the back substitution must count up (the M5 constraints). Stage 1 measured: the column's heat and salt conserved to rounding; a steady stress on a column of constant viscosity spins up the Ekman spiral with the transport τ/(ρ₀ f) at right angles to within 1 % and the surface current within 1° of the analytic spiral. Any one-step rotation followed by the implicit solve damps the steady state by f·dt/2 (the fixed point of the split sees an effective Coriolis parameter with a real part f²dt/2), 3 % at the ocean's 1350 s step; stage 2 decides whether the Coriolis term's existing treatment in the dynamics needs the same care.
6. **Diagnostics.** The mixed-layer depth becomes a diagnosed quantity (`mixedLayerDepth`: where the density first exceeds the surface layer's by 0.03 kg/m³, interpolated between layer centers), published for the page's `h1` and the logs, with the boundary-layer depth beside it.

Ice: layer 0 is clamped to freezing under ice and its excess heat goes to the ice base as today; the mixing scheme sees the ice's stress transmission. Skin: the cool skin stays; the warm layer's depth becomes min(3 m, h₀) with h₀ now 3 m, so stage 2 decides whether the warm layer is retired as redundant.

Open, outside the ocean: the equatorial wind response; in the ocean: the Southern Ocean's 240–310 m winter mixed layers (40–65S median 289–401 m against about 120–150 observed) and the 40–60S column 0.6 to 1.6 K warm at 100–500 m. |

Effort, from the panel's engineering judge: about 15 person-weeks in all, 6.5 to the stage-2 gate. There is no schedule; each stage is done when its tests pass.

## 6. Decisions taken, open points

Taken: continuous HYCOM-style hybrid rather than a hard seam at a fixed depth; 58 layers with 12 always-fixed surface layers; the minimum thickness cumulative per layer (HYCOM's rule), 10 m through the thermocline labels; the crossing search on the remap's parabolas with the mode-weighted flattening; PPM remap with a monotone limiter and no boundary extrapolation; the energetic boundary layer found by bisection on the linearized work with Kraus–Turner sources, Pacanowski–Philander below; convective adjustment as its own pass, its released energy the convective source; the pressure force at the depths the layers share, or at the gap's middle where they share none; the smaller edge thickness and the vanished layers pinned to the layer above; the PV floor at the layer's own minimum thickness; bottom drag left in the dynamics over the bottom `momentumDepth`; no lateral diffusion of the surface layer's heat by default (the slab's default `diffusivity` would put 3e4 m²/s on a 3 m layer); the slab path kept behind an option until the stage-5 gate.

Open, to be settled by test in stage 2: the deep minima (40 to 500 m) against the Southern Ocean's and the Arctic's stratification; the regrid rate limit τ_r; the minimum thickness h_min; m*'s rotational decay and the Langmuir cap against the coupled mixed-layer depths; the f·dt/2 damping of any split rotation; whether GM's surface taper is still needed; whether the warm-layer skin is retired; the seamount pressure-gradient error and whether the analytic finite-volume force is needed; the background diffusivity; whether columns shallower than about 100 m should use terrain-following targets for the fixed-depth part (HYCOM's σ-z-ρ) instead of vanishing layers at the floor, decided by a coastal-upwelling case in stage 2 against the pressure-gradient error over the shelf break; the treatment of the Red Sea and Mediterranean labels (1027.5 to 1028.0), which may become ordinary fixed-depth layers in those basins.
